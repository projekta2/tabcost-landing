How is the cost calculated?

The formula is very simple:

Cost per minute = (Hourly rate ÷ 60) × Impact percentage

  • Hourly rate: you set it (e.g., $50/h).
  • Impact percentage: default 20% (adjustable in Pro). It represents the realistic opportunity cost of an idle tab.

Example: Rate $50/h → $0.833/min → with 20% impact → $0.166 per minute of inactive tab. If you have 5 inactive tabs for 1 hour, the accumulated cost is ≈$0.83. Per day it could be $5‑10, per month more than $150 "lost" in opportunities.

TabCost only counts tabs that have been inactive beyond the grace period (default 5 minutes, adjustable in Pro). The active tab never generates cost.

Detailed behavior: why the counter sometimes "stops"

It's normal to see that the cost does not increase during long periods without interaction. This is not a bug, but a design feature of Chrome/Edge to save resources.

Why does the counter stop?

Short answer: Chrome suspends the extension when you don't use it to save battery and CPU.

Technical explanation: TabCost uses a service worker. If there is no interaction (you don't open the popup, you don't change tabs, you don't touch the browser), Chrome may stop it after a few seconds. When you interact again, the extension "wakes up" and recovers lost time, but only up to the last 15 minutes (default limit).

→ So, if you leave your computer for 2 hours without touching anything, when you return only the last 15 minutes of inactivity will be counted. This is intentional to avoid background resource consumption.

Why does the active tab never generate cost?

Because the extension understands that if you are looking at a tab, it is not "inactive". Only tabs that have not been visited beyond the grace period are penalized.

Why does a tab that was not counting start again after being abandoned?

When you click on a tab (or reload it), TabCost records that moment as the last interaction. If you then leave it, it will start counting from zero after the grace minutes. This is expected behavior and ensures that actively used tabs are not penalized.

Conclusion:

TabCost is accurate during normal activity (tab switching, browser usage). Only during prolonged inactivity does it apply the 15‑minute limit to protect performance. This is the standard way Chrome manages extensions.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

The extension doesn't count anything even though I have open tabs

Most likely cause: you haven't set an hourly rate > 0. Solution: open the popup and click "Set your rate".

Can I use TabCost without an internet connection?

Yes, 100%. All calculation and storage is local. Internet is only needed to validate the Pro license (once) and to open the Gumroad purchase page.

Are my data sent to any server?

No. Everything is stored in Chrome's local storage. We don't even use analytics. Your privacy is absolute.

Can I reset the daily cost before the day ends?

Yes, only in the Pro version. A ↺ button appears in the top right corner of the popup. Pressing it resets the current cost to zero without losing history.

Can auto‑close delete important tabs?

You can ignore domains (Pro) to prevent them from being auto‑closed (e.g., add "gmail.com", "drive.google.com"). Also, auto‑close is based on inactivity minutes, not content. You can always disable it by setting the value to 0.

I purchased a Pro license, how do I activate it?

In the popup or options page, enter the code you received by email (format XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX) and click "Activate". You need an internet connection once.

Why doesn't the total cost exactly match my hourly rate?

The impact percentage (20% by default) applies a reduction: it represents that an inactive tab does not cost you 100% of your rate, but a proportional part. You can adjust this percentage between 10% and 50% in Pro.

Does the extension slow down the browser?

No. It only performs calculations once per minute while the service worker is active. It is extremely lightweight.

How do I export the inactive tabs list to CSV?

If you are a Pro user, open the options panel and go to the "Critical Tabs Audit" section. You will see a blue button "Export inactive tabs to CSV". Clicking it will download a CSV file with all currently inactive tabs.

Tips to get the most out of TabCost

  • Be honest with your rate: Set your real market rate (or what you would like to earn).
  • Adjust grace minutes (Pro) to 3‑5 minutes so that a short break does not generate cost.
  • Ignore domains of music, messaging or passive tools if you don't want them to count (Pro).
  • Check the popup every few hours – seeing the number grow will motivate you to close what you don't use.
  • Use the "Release Focus" button to close all inactive tabs at once.
  • Export the inactive tabs list weekly to identify patterns.
  • Don't obsess – the cost is a metaphor for your attention, not a real bill.

Known limitations (by design)

  • The service worker may be suspended by Chrome → cost only updates during activity periods or up to 15 minutes of recovery.
  • History charts (Pro) show accumulated cost per day, not per hour.
  • It is not possible to measure cost when the browser is completely closed (logically).
  • The extension does not block or close tabs without your confirmation (except the optional auto‑close).
  • CSV export of inactive tabs includes all tabs that exceed the grace period, but not ignored domains.
